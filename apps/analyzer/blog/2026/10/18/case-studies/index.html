Case Study: Vishap on curl (cookie.c + http2.c)

October 18, 2026 · 2 min read

We pointed Vishap at two files from curl: lib/cookie.c and lib/http2.c. Between them it reported nine findings across five CWE classes. None is a proven vulnerability — these are triage candidates — but the shape of the results is worth writing down.

lib/cookie.c — one critical​

The critical finding is a data race (CWE-362): remove_expired and storecookie mutate the shared cookie list without synchronisation visible in the file. Whether that is exploitable depends on a share-lock contract defined elsewhere — which is exactly why the report says REQUIRES_REVIEW rather than asserting a vulnerability.

Three further findings: a NUL byte ends validation early in invalid_octets (CWE-20), an out-of-bounds read when indexing the cookie pieces array (CWE-125), and a partially initialised cookie left behind on allocation failure (CWE-459).

lib/http2.c — error paths​

The HTTP/2 file produced five findings, all on error paths; the happy path looked correct. Return values of stream initialisers go unchecked (CWE-252), cf_h2_ctx_free assumes a partially initialised context (CWE-459), a reachable DEBUGASSERT(0) compiles out to a silent NULL in release builds (CWE-617), and two optional items: window-size desync after a failed submit (CWE-754) and an unwritten single-thread contract (CWE-362).

What the reports looked like​

Each analysis produced the same structure: findings with CWE and severity, a STRIDE model tied to those findings, three refactoring options with effort estimates, and an audit trail with both the analysed-slice hash and the original material hash.

For cookie.c the recommended option was contract-hardening: fix the three local defects in two to three days and leave the locking architecture alone until someone proves concurrent access matters. For http2.c the same — the three important findings are all "propagate the failure" changes, not redesigns.

Read the full case studies: cookie.c and http2.c.

The honest caveats​

  • No proof of concept was built; nothing was fuzzed.
  • The material was truncated, which limits confidence in control flow across the cut.
  • Heuristic LLM analysis produces false positives. Some of these nine may be wrong, and some may already be known to curl's maintainers.

We drafted a disclosure note to security@curl.se describing both files. It is not sent yet.
